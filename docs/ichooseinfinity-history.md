# I Choose Infinity: the earlier record, 2013–2019

[Open record](../README.md) · [Historical page archive](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/README.md) · [Further original documents](../documents/supplementary.md)

**Historical guide assembled 17 October 2026.** I Choose Infinity fills an important part of the development between Luke's earlier working life and the current project family. Its surviving pages contain personal writing, teaching plans, proposed community organisations, technology designs, music, local Minjerribah ideas, public services and arguments about changing society.

Luke describes this period as an **“unstable epoch”**, before he worked out how to stabilise his emotions and find reliable pathways to **radical overcompliance and Joyful Responsible Abundance**. That is his framing supplied on **17 October 2026**. Read it together with the earlier pages: some ambitions continue, and his ways of understanding himself and pursuing those ambitions have changed.

The dates below distinguish an event described by Luke, a date written into a page, and the date the Internet Archive captured it. A capture establishes that a version was saved by that date. It does not establish when every paragraph was first written. The [archive index](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/README.md) records the available captures and gaps, with unchanged saved HTML and separate text extractions.

## The person speaking in the earlier pages

In the About Luke page captured on **19 July 2014**, he dates a change of direction to 2008. He says he became dissatisfied with his own apathy and his understanding of humanity's future, then undertook an immersive period of self-directed learning. His account connects that learning with strong emotions about government, corporations, money and the ability of ordinary people to shape their lives. He also acknowledges debts and difficult effects on other people during the process.

The same page is candid about difficulty asking for help, especially financial help. He wanted others to offer assistance because asking had been difficult and often unsuccessful. Those circumstances help explain why invitations to collaborators and access to resources recur throughout the site. They also belong beside his more confident claims about what he thought he had understood.

Source: [About Luke, captured 19 July 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140719055124/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/community/about-luke/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140719055124-community-about-luke.html.txt).

**My Hopes and Fears**, captured on **11 May 2014**, is a useful short introduction to his personal wishes. He wanted wellbeing, an active life, regular travel and learning through experience, and opportunities to compose and perform meaningful music. He wanted other people to help define their own futures as well. His listed fears included ridicule and asking for assistance or resources. The page also expressed opposition to money, statutory rules, nation states, corporations, pollution and incomplete product cycles. These are his positions in that earlier text; his October 2026 account of radical overcompliance supplies a later point in the development.

Source: [My Hopes and Fears, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511061023/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/my-hopes-and-fears/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511061023-my-hopes-and-fears.html.txt).

The biography captured on **1 April 2016** adds a more everyday account. Luke wrote about growing up in Queensland, poetry, music, the ocean and bodyboarding. He described beginning a game-design diploma at Evocca College in April 2015 to communicate with game and virtual-reality developers and manage IT projects. He wanted collaborators who could complement weaknesses he identified in money management, procrastination, stable housing and a suitable workplace. He reported living on approximately $200 per week in AUStudy and said he did not come from an affluent background.

His account at that time also describes being single, heterosexual and polyamorous, living with three other people in Spring Hill. He connected a preference for shared access to resources with the practical freedom to learn, create and contribute. The page asks for encouragement and collaboration in turning the larger vision into physical work. It also expresses a wish to introduce himself through an interview with Kerry O'Brien. These details belong to the dated 2016 account.

## By December 2013: a course leading into community action

The earliest substantive page successfully recovered in the first network snapshot is the **16 December 2013 home page**. It organises I Choose Infinity into a learning sequence about whole-system change. The proposed first day introduces a conversation and video-based perspectives. The second moves into local and global community projects, technical projects, events, choosing a direction and recruiting people for specific roles. A six-week community challenge follows, with the goal of creating useful work and local opportunities, then designing a preferred way of life.

This structure matters to the later work. Learning, reflection, public conversation, practical projects and a life worth choosing already form one process. The site asks visitors to work through the material with friends, take notes and discuss it. Its early invitation is social and participatory as well as individual.

The **22 February 2014** version adds a more specific connection between local participation and livelihood: regional crowdfunding and work-away arrangements, with food and housing exchanged for people's time. The names and mechanisms differ from the later Community Hours, Ready S.E.T. and Mutual Futures work. The recurring question is how people can access resources, contribute, learn and live without every useful action being constrained by an immediate shortage of money.

## February 2014: Verse Box, public conversation and community music

In **Open Letter to Everyone**, Luke says he attended **Hack the Evening at The Edge, State Library of Queensland, on 13 February 2014** to discuss prototyping a walk-in computer. His mother had suggested its name, **Verse Box**.

The design brought cameras, microphones, projectors, lights, switches, computers and a Wi-Fi server into a small room. An external community noticeboard would connect to additional information through a smartphone app and augmented-reality overlays. Inside, participants would record their opinions, ideas, emotions and values. Luke wanted structured questions to help make a diverse collection of responses understandable and usable in a continuing public conversation.

The letter names a proposed collective digital perspective, **Lynx Destiny Infinity**, developed from participants' values and experiences. It also connects the conversation to music. Communities would develop verse and songs from their own hopes and opinions, contributing to a proposed 40-hour global concert during the **15–16 November 2014 Brisbane G20 summit**. Recording an event from many viewpoints would support later virtual revisiting and new ways to experience it.

Several later threads are visible here: a space for recorded personal reflection, a growing personal or collective model, community participation, creative expression and connected local gatherings. Verse Box also gives the archive a tangible design, with proposed components and a stated prototyping setting. The letter records an invitation and a development proposal. Delivery of a prototype or concert has to be established through further records.

Source: [Open Letter to Everyone, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060505/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/open-letter-to-everyone/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060505-open-letter-to-everyone.html.txt).

The separately recovered **Verse Box** page specifies a proposed booth measuring three metres by one and a half metres, operated through voice and body movements. It gives participants a choice between anonymous and attributed opinions and asks about ownership, storage and sharing of information. Participants could suggest changes to questions and prompts. Its access and development proposal called for free public use and reciprocal, noncommercial development. Those are the terms proposed on that historical page; the current repositories carry their own dated licences.

**Verse Box Pattern** develops the physical and conversational design further. It seeks off-the-shelf or recycled components so remote communities could adapt it. Cameras and microphones could be switched off for an anonymous mode. A participant would see the transcription, correct misunderstood words and identify keywords. Volunteers would review the resulting opinions while the system developed its pattern recognition. The intended node combines community conversation with possible environmental, health, arts and music uses.

Sources: [Verse Box, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511061053/http://ichooseinfinity.com/verse-box/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511061053-verse-box.html.txt); [Verse Box Pattern, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060536/http://ichooseinfinity.com/verse-box-pattern/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060536-verse-box-pattern.html.txt). These two page payloads were recovered from the existing local Wayback cache.

**Plan 03** elaborates a network of public opinion rooms and noticeboards, connected through local Wi-Fi and accessible later from a person's own computer or phone. It includes assumptions about participation, costs and throughput and treats the shared network as a place for continuing questions and decisions. Those are the calculations and expectations of that proposal. The more recent community-compute papers develop another version of the practical infrastructure question.

Source: [Plan 03, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060831/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/plan-03/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060831-plan-03.html.txt).

## 2014: law as inspectable information

The **Australian Law** page proposes organising laws through data about their creation and consequences. Luke lists proposals, contributors, parliamentary votes, amendments, lobbying, costs, enforcement and effects on communities. He wants people to ask which people and organisations influenced rules, who benefited, who was harmed and how those relationships changed over time.

The proposed method is to collect relevant information, connect the necessary measures and develop visualisations that reveal patterns more clearly than separate documents. This gives the contemporary legal and civic work a specific historical foundation. The later legal-retrieval designs, legal-memory workbench and evidence-linked public decision systems develop tools around a question he was already asking: how can people understand the rules and the processes that shape their lives?

Source: [Australian Law, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060414/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/australian-law/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060414-australian-law.html.txt).

The About Luke and My Hopes and Fears pages also contain explicit statements rejecting parts of the legal and monetary order. The historical record preserves that opposition. Luke's **17 October 2026** explanation identifies stabilising his emotions and finding reliable routes into radical overcompliance as changes in how he pursues Joyful Responsible Abundance. A full account should carry both the earlier argument and his stated change of approach.

## 2014: personal digital reflections, interfaces and shared models

**Patterns of Consciousness**, captured on **19 July 2014**, proposes a digital guide or mirror organised through a person's values, questions, beliefs and experiences. Luke experiments with language, geometric forms and mathematical relationships as ways to organise such a system. He imagines a person using the reflection to explore existing knowledge, make choices and develop their own direction.

These are his philosophical and design ideas in that page. They connect directly to the personal reflection process in the [2014 Constitutional Matrix](../documents/supplementary/The_Constitutional_Matrix_of_Participation_2014.pdf), the [July 2023 Aura presentation](../documents/supplementary/Aura_of_Intelligence_Version_7_July_2023.pdf) and the later Aura work. The earlier material helps readers understand why ethics, emotional life, geometry, interface design and artificial intelligence recur together.

Source: [Patterns of Consciousness, captured 19 July 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140719055227/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/patterns-of-consciousness/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140719055227-patterns-of-consciousness.html.txt).

The **Questions** page makes the conversational idea particularly clear. It scripts an exchange with **Lynx Destiny Infinity**, beginning with the participant's choice of anonymous or attributed participation. The dialogue asks about identity, personal values and the values the participant wants the digital perspective to develop. It then offers subjects ranging from personal experiences and needs through local environment, governance, health, information ownership and lifelong learning.

Luke presents this as a process in which participants help shape an evolving digital representation of humanity while seeking help to develop their own lives. This is direct evidence of the 2014 design, preserved in a contemporary page rather than reconstructed from a later description. The page's propositions about consciousness and collective intelligence remain the author's philosophical and design claims.

Source: [Questions, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060526/http://ichooseinfinity.com/questions/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060526-questions.html.txt).

**Patterns of Technology** links that interest to interfaces available or being developed at the time: EEG headsets, eye tracking, virtual reality, connected devices, optical communications and environmental data. Luke's proposed combination is a model through which people could explore data about society, the environment and themselves, and rehearse possible changes.

**Patterns of Observation** similarly assembles environmental and space-weather datasets into a proposed layered view. It mixes links to scientific observations with Luke's broader interpretations and speculative questions. The useful continuity is the design direction: bring different streams together, make relationships inspectable and let people explore them. Specific causal or technical statements retain the context of the earlier page.

Sources: [Patterns of Technology, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060818/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/patterns-of-technology/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060818-patterns-of-technology.html.txt); [Patterns of Observation, captured 11 May 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140511060813/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/patterns-of-observation/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140511060813-patterns-of-observation.html.txt).

## 2014: learning, teaching, paid work and recreation

**Potential Collaborators** proposes people choosing projects, roles and the amount of time they can contribute. Its suggested roster combines learning, teaching, earning and recreation. Luke asks people to work on projects they consider worthwhile and describes each participant's voice as having equal worth. The page also identifies weekly visits to The Edge as a place to develop campaigns, media, websites and event patterns together.

**Time Share Roster** gives that balance an explicit pattern: **30% learning, 20% teaching, 30% recreation and 20% business**. It asks participants to choose their hours, income aspirations, preferred setting and whether they want to work alone or with others. They then identify skills to learn and teach, activities they enjoy and possible sources of income before turning those choices into a calendar. The freedom to organise a life, and the relationship between learning, work and enjoyable time, are already part of the design.

The **Digital Marketing Campaigner** challenge turns some of this into a sequence of practical tasks. Participants would develop websites and local media, live streaming, newsletters, mailing lists, augmented-reality material, crowdfunding and analytics. They would then record training videos, learn from each other and swap roles. A related three-month plan seeks collaborators, workspaces, equipment and income to build the first set of shared services.

The **Training Schedule**, labelled March to May 2014, lists software and electronics work for Verse Box alongside media, radio, events, research, safety, art, music and translation. It proposes iterative training videos and periodic reviews that condense experience into quicker learning pathways for later participants. It provides a more detailed view of the intended team than an isolated project description.

These pages are useful predecessors to Ready S.E.T., Community Hours and the teacher programme. They connect choosing a role, acquiring skills, teaching other people and creating paid work. The older pages contain their own recruitment and earnings expectations; they record what Luke proposed at that time.

## 2014: access to health knowledge and tools

**Scientific Self Analysis** proposes an Institute of Life where people could learn about themselves and gain access to health-related technologies. Luke's list ranges from basic monitoring and chemistry to imaging, DNA sequencing and other research tools. The page combines objections to barriers in health care with a proposal for more direct learning and access.

This supplies another earlier thread leading toward the later body-and-mind twin, Geode and dementia-care research proposals. The original expresses his frustration and ambitions in 2014. The [dementia clinical-research first draft](../documents/supplementary/Clinical_Research_Path_for_Aura_of_Dementia_First_Draft.pdf) and later Genesis documents develop more detailed research and operating questions. The historical page records a proposed institute and learning environment.

## June–July 2014: a connected Minjerribah proposal

The **NSI Community Produce Garden** page ties food, ecology, information, energy, care and manufacturing to specific questions about island life. It considers vertical growing systems, an ecological survey and public digital archive, community discussion before major development, local Wi-Fi, health alerts for older people, assisted transport and research into useful island materials. It dates a walk through Amity to **3 June 2014**, when Luke was considering possible locations and how to explain the idea.

His sequence begins with understanding the environment and connecting residents, then developing decisions and physical projects through shared information. That pattern recurs in the Minjerribah living twin, the multi-site network, care proposals, learning spaces, clean-energy work and Civilisation of Sand. The page also includes images of the proposed food tower and local network arrangements and links to a spreadsheet index of the then-current site.

**NSI Community Events** treats gatherings as a means to bring people, skills, research tools and training to the island while improving local services. It proposes connecting practical needs with themed events, learning and ongoing local activity. The ambitions include the familiar wider horizon of advanced materials, communication, sustainability and long-term life possibilities.

The designs, cost estimates and claims about particular technologies belong to the 2014 proposals. Their historical value is also local and personal: Luke was already trying to connect island problems with research, public learning, media, visiting collaborators and access to the tools to build things.

## July 2014: an earlier political proposal

The surviving **Blog by Luke** post proposes an **I Choose Infinity Political Party of Australia** and explicitly says that the party had not been registered. Its programme connects freely accessible digital education, a shared knowledge commons, personal reflection, ecosystem research, power, materials, translation, international cooperation and participatory governance.

This is an earlier political expression within the history that now includes P4A. The post also asks people to examine Luke's online history and offers time to collaborators interested in making a digital reflection or biography. He acknowledges having hurt people and having done things he would not repeat, while describing what he wanted to build next. Those statements belong in the record alongside the policy ambitions.

Its proposed referendum and transition dates are dates in the 2014 argument. The post establishes a published proposal and an invitation to organise. It does not establish subsequent registration or an electoral outcome.

Source: [Blog by Luke, captured 19 July 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20140719055107/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/blog-by-luke/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20140719055107-blog-by-luke.html.txt).

## 2014–2016: versions, travel and a practical portfolio

The **17 December 2014** home page introduces a revised form of the site. It describes connected learning, truthful answers, environmental regeneration, communication across languages and a body of information organised around good conscience. Its phrase about patterns of sudden life gaming is an early link to Luke's later life-design material.

By **11 March 2015**, the home page says version 1 had lapsed after roughly a year online and had contained more than 150 pages. Luke says he retained XML saves and substantial offline material. The page identifies a focus on compassionate global responsible abundance, links to earlier blogs and autobiography, and says he was beginning to travel again. That statement is a useful explanation for gaps between a large earlier site and later surviving versions, as well as a route for further archival work.

Sources: [home, 17 December 2014](https://web.archive.org/web/20141217165438/http://ichooseinfinity.com/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20141217165438-home.html.txt); [home, 11 March 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20150311052201/http://ichooseinfinity.com:80/) · [saved text](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/pages/20150311052201-home.html.txt).

Luke says he registered the domain in 2013 and describes the current site as a portfolio, personal blog and guide for customers and collaborators. He lists WordPress development, social media, intelligent automation, custom training, business-plan evaluation, marketing and outsourcing advice. ## What carries forward, and what changed

Several enduring concerns can be followed directly through these sources: people defining the lives they want; personal digital reflections; a shared conversation informed by values; practical learning and meaningful work; local access to technology; public understanding of rules; music and gatherings; ecological care; and an ambitious future beyond present limitations.

The record also contains change. Earlier pages express rejection of institutions and strong certainty about proposed solutions. Later documents develop specifications, evidence records, permissions, responsibilities, research questions and routes through existing law. Luke's own October 2026 framing connects that development with learning to stabilise his emotions and finding reliable pathways to radical overcompliance and Joyful Responsible Abundance.

The [2022 Motivation & Toolbox](../documents/supplementary/Lukes_2022_Motivation_and_Toolbox.pdf), [2023 Aura presentation](../documents/supplementary/Aura_of_Intelligence_Version_7_July_2023.pdf), [current alignment paper](../documents/originals/Joyful_Responsible_Abundance_Human_and_SI_Alignment_Revised.pdf) and [September 2026 Community Hours plan](../documents/originals/C-Hour-Joyful-Responsible-Abundance-Plan.pdf) provide later points in that sequence. Read them with the history, retaining each source's date, purpose and language.

## Scope of this recovery

The preserved CDX index was requested for **2013–2019** and contains **399 capture records**. It is a discovery index, not a claim that all those responses, linked files, media or site versions have been recovered. It includes earlier November 2013 entries and a January 2019 favicon entry. There is currently **no successfully recovered substantive 2019 page** in this collection.

The [original site-catalogue spreadsheet](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/downloads/new-ichooseinfinity-index-2014.xlsx) is also preserved unchanged. Its descriptions are labelled **5 June 2014**, while the archive capture is dated **13 June 2014**. It has three sheets and fields for page names, descriptions, priorities, categories and text/video lengths. The Community Produce Garden page describes it as an index to 129 pages. It provides a contemporary route through the site alongside the later historical guide.

The archive combines successful network recovery with identifiable locally saved Wayback pages where available. Saved Wayback replay copies may already contain archive banners, rewritten links or changes made by the earlier offline saving process. The manifest distinguishes those from newly recovered original-response HTML. Each saved HTML file is copied unchanged, with a checksum and its source capture URL. Separate text files aid reading and searching. Scripts have not been executed as part of extraction or verification.

The [historical archive index](../documents/historical/ichooseinfinity/README.md) gives the actual recovered counts, per-page provenance, successful but uninformative responses, and gaps. The prose above mostly paraphrases the dated sources. The preserved originals let readers follow the language and detail for themselves.
