Encoding Music

A Pattern For Writing Meaningful Music

Step 1: Decide upon the message you wish to inspire

Having a message that many people can immediately relate to is extremely important if you want your music to be meaningful and popular. Your message can be very simple, or you can entwine it within a longer story to deliver multiple meanings and messages.

Step 2: Decide upon the emotions you wish to inspire

The objective of a good story, verse or song is to connect to the listener by inspiring their emotions to well up within them, which in turn may bring forth memories or empower new memories. The more you empower the listeners positive emotions, the more likely they will enjoy it and attach it to new memories.

Step 3: Decide if you wish to encode deeper meaning

Deeper meaning can be subtle so that any listener will understand, or it can be hidden deep within the masterpiece so that only the initiated (& the subconscious) will notice.

Step 4: Decide on the complexity of deeper meaning

To do this you have to think of what you want to encode and how to encode it. There are an infinite number of ways that you can do this and this list will give you a starting point. You can use combinations of symbols, numbers, letters, words, sounds, images, silence.

Step 5: Experiment until you have a solid framework

Use brainstorming techniques to go through steps 1 through 4 at least twice, I do it three times, you will begin to see patterns emerge from your creations. Keep experimenting until you identify the pattern you want for your song and/or your whole album.

Step 6: Experience/feel emotions you wish to inspire

Before you move into your chosen emotion, prepare your instruments and/or workspace. If you are not a very emotional person you can assist yourself into an emotional state by watching a movie, looking at photographs, listening to music or by using any item, place or thought as a permission slip to enter your chosen emotional state.

Step 7: Write or play music patterns while emotional

Once you have entered your chosen emotional state you should begin to compose your music. Now enter your story and use the power of your emotions to guide you through. Music and emotions are intimately connected and they both take patience and practice to master, so don’t rush yourself.

Step 8: Record patterns as a script or by microphone

I like to follow the mathematical pattern of 1,1,2,3,5,8,13… when in this stage of songcraft. By this I mean that I record small patterns of music in multiple sections called “Bars.” A Bar is a length of time in which a certain number of beats are counted. 4 beat Bar is 4 seconds long. Using this pattern I record 10 individual Bars per each number. Â Which means I write 1 Bar x 10 patterns, 1 Bar x 10, 2 Bar x 10, 3 Bar x 10, 5 Bar x 10, 8 Bar x 10, and 13 Bar x 10 =
4 seconds x 10, 4 sec x 10, 8 sec x 10, 12 sec x 10, 20 sec x 10, 32 sec x 10, and 52 sec x 10

Consider Volume, Speed/Tempo, Pitch, Tone, Repetition, Silence, Beginning, Middle, End.

Step 9: Mix and match your new patterns into Verse

By the time you have written the 15 or 20 minutes of sound bites, once again patterns will begin to emerge from your creations. If you have access to a microphone and a computer with editing software this stage is quite easy. Cut an past your patterns together and shape your chosen patterns into song.

Step 10: Evolve your message to build synchronicity

In many cases you will have to revisit your message and evolve it with the music you have crafted. I like to revise my songs at least 3 times to ensure I am happy with the patterns.

Step 11: Review and evolve till finished, then repeat
