Local Fresh Food

How could we produce all of the fresh food required for the entire population of people and visitors to Minjerribah, on the Island in a sustainable way?

Is it possible to, in the same process remove all reliance on the mainland for food, drink, electricity, communication, transport, learning, health, recycling and governance?

The island is gifted with the raw materials required to build many modern technologies, so what is really needed is the belief that the island and the people here, deserve to live in a completely self sustainable and highly efficient society. The sands are rich with minerals, that can be quickly prepared to use with 3D printers to build computers, camera lenses, buildings, and titanium products such as bone replacements, medical devices and more.

Focus on Symbiosis

With a sensitive island environment it is not a great idea to clear large chunks of land to develop traditional agricultural food production. Instead, one or more deeply researched food towers should be developed to protect the environment and produce amazing food.

Imagine sampling all of the plant and animal life in the ecosystem, to understand all of the processes and connections of the web. Then condense all of those processes into vertical towers to study and replenish the already depleted environment. What we learn from this process will lead to being able to produce huge amounts of organic food, while reversing the pressure on the local environment until it is at full strength again.

A vertical food tower will need quite a bit of energy for lighting, cooking and so on as there is not enough sunlight to provide the most efficient and high capacity production required to feed thousands of people as well as replenish the depleted local environment.

Lucky the Rainbow Channel has enough tidal water flow to produce clean and sustainable power by using dutch designed Dynamic Tidal Power systems. While the Dutch/Chinese example uses bridges, there are many other ways to customize tidal power technology to minimize environmental impact, reverse the erosion of Amity Point, power the entirety of Minjerribah with excess power left over, and still avoid building a bridge to the mainland.

Much more research is needed but a brief walk through the area yesterday 03 June 2014 to observe the natural environment, did empower me to construct the image below so I may describe 2 ideal places for one or two food farm towers in Amity.

There is a diverse series of ecosystems in this region and during the planning process every plant, fungi, bacteria and animal should be analyzed and documented into a digital archive for the entire community to view and interact with. I am aware of a system called “Leaf Snap” which could be investigated to achieve this. Click Here for Leaf Snap Website.

There is a process of evolution which must occur with the entire community coming together to define what food they wish to eat, how it will be distributed, how it will be served and eaten, how it will be tended, and of course how and where it will be built.

In my personal belief it is essential on a project of this complexity, to develop a free digital information distribution network before any of the major commencement decisions are made. This way, the whole community can participate in real time discussion and ongoing surveys through a Wifi Local Area Network. (A mini internet)

For example:

This WiFi SWS Server can facilitate up to 50 simultaneous video streaming downloads or up to 200 simultaneous non-video information downloads within a diameter of about 500 meters, depending on the environmental conditions. Cost price from the manufacturer of these devices in China is $400 USD + Shipping, with a minimum purchase of 2 Units.

These devices are capable of distributing information over 3 different layers of network protocols which can be arranged for different streams of information. Additional layers can be added in the future by adding new devices more specialized for salty beach air and specific needs established via the community coming to consensus using the first 3 layers.

Did you know that the sand minerals being mined on Minjerribah could be manufactured into computers and solar panels and much more, if enough people decided to research the engineering process and work together to acquire 3D printers to make it happen?

In a community like Amity Point on Minjerribah, to provide the entire population with this WiFi Local Area Network, the community would require a minimum of 8 devices which are as evenly distributed as possible.

For all settlement areas on Minjerribah, to provide the entire population with this WiFi Local Area Network, the community would require a minimum of 43 devices which are as evenly distributed as possible, within each settlement.

You may have thought, what do we need a Local Area Network for?

First of all there is the aging population on the island. How to care for so many people if many are disconnected from the community? Could we implement a health alert system with computer assisted consultation and life sign monitoring? Could we also implement a sustainable semi-autonomous transport network so more people can enjoy mobility?

Each new use for the system will require a customized layer of computerized devices, similar to to the first layer mentioned above. With 3D printers and a team of people willing to learn materials science and computer aided drawing, the raw sand minerals can be converted into these devices. This lowers cost while increases strength and reliability.
